eSIM vs roaming vs a local SIM: which is cheapest when you travel?

The real differences between your carrier's roaming, a travel eSIM and a local SIM card, and when each one makes sense.

Roaming with your own carrier

The easiest option: nothing to install. It is usually the most expensive per GB, and daily roaming passes can add up quickly on a longer trip. Check your carrier's exact price before you fly.

A travel eSIM

You buy a data plan online and install it on your phone before you leave, usually in a couple of minutes. Your own SIM stays in the phone, so your number and WhatsApp keep working. For most trips this is the cheapest way to get data, and you are online the moment you land.

  • Your phone must support eSIM and be unlocked (dial *#06# and look for an EID)
  • Most travel eSIMs are data only; calls and texts go through apps like WhatsApp
  • Install it on Wi-Fi at home, and switch mobile data to it when you land

A local SIM card

Sometimes the cheapest for long stays in one country, but you need to find a shop, sometimes show your passport, and swap out your own SIM, which means losing your home number while it's out.

So which should you choose?

  • Short or multi-country trip: a travel eSIM is usually the best value
  • Long stay in one country with lots of data: compare a local SIM with a large eSIM plan
  • Just a day or two and very little data: roaming can be fine
